
# Rule:

> Never ask “What can I build well?”
> Ask “What pain is someone already tolerating badly?”

* Pain > possibility

* Friction > features

* Irritation > innovation

---

# Rule:

> If it doesn’t change what users do, it doesn’t matter how well it’s built.

ask:

* What exact behavior stops?

* What new habit starts?

* What action becomes easier by ≥30%?

---

# Rule:

> Architectural rigor must be earned by usage, not imagined futures.

Use this ladder:

* Works

* Used

* Repeatedly used

* Relied upon

* Only now → Hardened, scalable, secure

---

# Rule:

> Early decisions must be cheap to undo, not perfect.

Ask:

* Can I delete this in a day?

* Can I rewrite this in a week?

* Can I pivot this without rewriting everything?


---

# Rule:

> If you can’t describe one user vividly, you’re designing for nobody.

> Engineering becomes sharp when the user is sharp.

---

# Rule:

> Never compete with AI on generation. Compete on selection.

Your value is:

* Deciding what to build

* Deciding what not to build

* Deciding when to harden

* Deciding where quality actually matters

---

# Rule:

> Compliments don’t matter. Reuse does.

Look for:

* Users returning without reminders

* People asking for missing features

* Workarounds forming organically

* Others building on top of your solution

---

# Rule:

> Do not let resentment toward sloppy products poison your judgment.

Poorly engineered products often win because:

* They were early

* They were simple

* They fit human behavior

---

# Before you build anything, ask:

> “If this succeeds, what will people rely on—and if it fails, what did I learn fast?”
